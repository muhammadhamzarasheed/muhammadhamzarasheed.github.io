Rest is not a reward

Muhammad Hamza · Assessment Lead · August 2026 · 3 minute read

I will rest once it is all over. That is what I told myself for three years, through an MSc in Accounting and Finance, ACCA exams and an Advanced Diploma, all while working alongside the study. Life became an endless cycle of deadlines, and at one point I genuinely started to believe that stress was simply part of my personality.

Then, gradually, the pile shrank. The diploma finished. The MSc finished, with Merit. The ACCA papers stacked up to finalist level. A new role arrived. And for the first time in years, there was room to breathe.

I had been treating rest as a reward: something you collect at the finish line, once the work is done and the results are in. The problem is that for anyone building something, the finish line keeps moving. There is always a next exam, a next deadline, a next board. If rest only comes after the work, it never comes.

You do not earn rest. You need it to keep going.

Through those years, travelling was my escape. Even when everything felt overwhelming, I made sure to get out, see somewhere new, and reset. Looking back, those breaks were not time taken away from the work. They were what made the work sustainable. My honest regret is not that I rested too much. It is that I did not rest more.

There is a related lesson I learned the slow way. Not every season can be fixed by working harder. Some situations remain unchanged after you have done everything you can, and what they ask of you is not another plan but the strength to carry on while the answer has not arrived yet. Carrying anything for a long time requires strength, and strength requires rest. Showing up when motivation is gone is only possible if you have not already spent everything.

So if you are grinding towards something and wondering whether you deserve a break, you are asking the wrong question. Deserving does not come into it. Take the break. Travel if you can. Disconnect properly.

Success will come. Survive long enough to enjoy it when it does.
